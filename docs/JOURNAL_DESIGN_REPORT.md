# Phase 3 journal design and editorial readiness

The existing palette, Playfair Display/Open Sans, responsive site shell, portrait, 27 service URLs, 12 article URLs, five videos and Google Forms architecture remain intact. The journal receives its own editorial composition rather than duplicating the site.

## Reader journey

The landing page combines an asymmetric introduction and featured read, approved illustrative photography, seven topic links, title/summary search with optional category filter, a full article collection, three topic collections, an authentic reviewer portrait/profile link, related services and the existing appointment CTA. Cards use restrained colour and numbered editorial marks, avoiding the same stock photo twelve times. Primary content and links work without JavaScript.

Categories reconcile legacy metadata through explicit journalCategory, preserving article URLs. Only populated categories have routes; fewer than two public articles forces noindex even if the production switch changes later. Collections are not new duplicate article bodies.

The article layout provides clear category breadcrumbs, an actual editorial publisher/author, separate medical reviewer and AI-assistance disclosure, genuine dates, estimated reading time, compact illustrative image, contextualized key takeaways, readable paragraphs, native reviewed FAQs, references, curated related care/reading and the existing educational disclaimer. Existing urgent-care wording is elevated for ectopic, high-risk and bleeding articles without editing the recommendation.

Anchor contents appear only when the guide is long enough and has sufficient sections. Desktop contents stay outside the reading column; mobile contents are a native disclosure. A lightweight copy-link control preserves the actual preview URL and falls back to a selectable field. Print styles retain content, attribution, references and disclaimer. Native MDX tables/figures/notices are supported, with documented accessible wrappers. A reading-progress indicator was omitted because these concise guides do not benefit from another moving/sticky element.

## Credibility and governance

The reviewer page shows only approved professional information and actual reviewed/authored relationships. No AI article is relabelled as personally doctor-written. Registration remains absent because it was not supplied. Review records remain separate from body copy; their URLs are not emitted in public HTML, search JSON or schema. The public editorial policy explains the roles and limits in patient-friendly language.

FAQ knowledge separates appointment administration, general educational discovery, service topics and article questions. A shared component/identity avoids drift; the repeated appointment question is removed from all clinical blocks and centralized. Existing clinical answers are not rewritten. Related content uses explicit topic relationships and published records; cosmetic article gaps are retained rather than filled with arbitrary links.

## SEO/GEO/AIO foundations for the next phase

Static summaries/headings, accessible taxonomy, reviewer/author transparency, real dates, canonical breadcrumbs, relevant reciprocal links and source references improve interpretability. BlogPosting and MedicalWebPage now carry visible category and reviewer profile URLs. Dates are unchanged; no fake credentials, ratings or FAQ rich-result promises. Static search contains public title/summary/category/URL fields only. Search terms stay in browser memory; there is no external search service, storage or patient-search tracking.

Production canonicals and the existing indexing switch remain. GitHub Pages project-base handling is preserved for pages, anchors, assets, script fetches and search URLs. Phase 4 has not started; no ranking/AI placement or field performance claims are made.
