## What does “abnormal uterine bleeding” mean?

It simply means bleeding that is different from the usual menstrual pattern. For example:
- Your period has become much heavier than before.
- You notice bleeding between periods.
- Bleeding happens after sex.
- Your period lasts longer than a week.
- You notice any vaginal bleeding after menopause.

An occasional change does not automatically mean something serious. But repeated, unusually heavy or unexpected bleeding should not be ignored.

## Why might periods change?

There are several possible reasons, including changes in hormones, growths such as fibroids, pregnancy-related conditions, certain medicines and other health problems. You cannot reliably tell the cause from the amount of bleeding alone.

## When should you book a consultation?

Arrange medical assessment if bleeding is unusually heavy, continues longer than expected, happens between periods, or is accompanied by pain or tiredness. Any bleeding after menopause needs assessment.

**Get urgent medical help:** If you are soaking through pads very rapidly and feel dizzy, faint, breathless or have chest pain, seek emergency medical care rather than waiting for a routine appointment. If pregnancy is possible and you have severe pain or heavy bleeding, seek urgent care.

## What should you tell the gynecologist?

You can write down:
1. The first day of each period and how long it lasts.
2. Whether bleeding is light, moderate or heavy.
3. Whether you have pain, clots or bleeding between periods.
4. Any pregnancy possibility, medicines or contraceptives you use.

A gynecologist may discuss your history, examine you with your consent and recommend tests if appropriate. Not everyone needs the same tests.
