## What is menopause?

**Menopause** is the stage when menstrual periods have stopped for 12 consecutive months, without another cause such as pregnancy or a medical condition. The time leading up to it is called **perimenopause**. During perimenopause, periods and symptoms can change.

## What symptoms might you notice?

Some people have few symptoms. Others notice:
- Periods coming earlier, later or less regularly.
- Sudden feelings of heat, often called **hot flashes** or **hot flushes**.
- Sweating at night and disturbed sleep.
- Changes in mood or difficulty concentrating.
- Vaginal dryness or discomfort.
- Bladder changes and needing the toilet more often.

Not everyone experiences all these changes. Symptoms can also have causes other than menopause.

## When should you talk to a gynecologist?

Consider booking an appointment if symptoms interfere with sleep, work or daily life; if you are unsure why periods have changed; or if you would like to discuss care options.

**Important:** Any vaginal bleeding after you have gone 12 months without periods needs medical assessment. Do not assume that it is simply part of menopause.

## Can menopause symptoms be managed?

Often, yes. Options depend on your symptoms, medical history and preferences. They may include changes to daily habits and, for some people, medical treatments. A clinician can explain the benefits and risks of suitable options. Avoid starting hormone treatments based on an online article alone.

## How to prepare for your appointment

Keep a short note of period dates, night sweats, sleep changes, medicines and any questions. You do not need to remember everything during the visit.
