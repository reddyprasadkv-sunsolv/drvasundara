## What is urinary incontinence?

**Urinary incontinence** means urine leaks when you did not intend it to.

Two common patterns are:
- **Leakage with pressure:** A little urine escapes when you cough, sneeze, lift something heavy or exercise.
- **Leakage with urgency:** You suddenly need to urinate and may leak before reaching the toilet.

Some people experience both. The cause cannot be diagnosed from a blog post.

## Why does it happen?

Pregnancy and childbirth, age-related changes, menopause, certain health conditions and other factors may play a role. Sometimes a bladder infection or medicine contributes. The right assessment depends on your symptoms.

## When should you seek medical advice?

Speak to a healthcare professional if leakage limits your activities, disturbs sleep or makes you worry about leaving home.

**Seek prompt medical attention** if you cannot pass urine, notice blood in your urine, have painful urination or develop significant pelvic pain. Some symptoms may need urgent assessment.

## Are there treatments?

Many people can benefit from appropriate care. Depending on the cause, a clinician might suggest bladder training, pelvic floor muscle exercises, lifestyle changes, medicines or other treatments. Surgery is not necessary for everyone.

Do not stop drinking water completely to avoid leaks. Ask a healthcare professional how much fluid is appropriate for you.

## Preparing for a consultation

Write down when leakage happens, how often you urinate, whether you have pain, and any medicines you take. A simple bladder diary can help the clinician understand what is happening.
